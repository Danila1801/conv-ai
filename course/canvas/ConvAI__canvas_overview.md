2) Course overview

Learning outcomes

Knowledge and understanding: At the end of the course, students will have a solid understanding of conversational AI concepts (e.g., dialogue acts), development and evaluation of dialogue systems, large language model (LLM) architectures and how LLMs can be deployed as conversational agents, challenges with LLMs as conversational agents and mitigation strategies, grounding LLMs to external knowledge, improving their reliability through search, reasoning and verification, and emerging topics such as reinforcement learning and multimodality.

Applying knowledge: Students will be able to implement conversational AI using LLMs and test their system on various conversational tasks.

Making judgments: Students will increase their ability to evaluate/reflect on the technical capabilities and limitations of current conversational AI.

Communication skills: Students will write a scientific report on an original research question in collaboration with 1-2 teammates. Students will be able to explain a recent publication in conversational AI.

Learning skills: Students will gain experience in independent/self-directed learning by developing conversational AI for a realistic challenge, synthesizing an original and precise research question, and performing the corresponding experiments to answer it.

Course content

Foundations of conversational AI, such as dialogue acts, and history of conversational AI approaches.

Concepts and architectures of Large Language models as components of conversational AI, including Transformers, attention, and embeddings.

Evaluation of conversational AI and distilling their strengths and limitations

Enrichment with background knowledge from documents and knowledge graphs

Search, reasoning, and verification of LLM-based conversational AI

Emerging topics of conversational AI, such as agentic AI, multimodality, reinforcement learning

Prerequisites

1. Python programming: at least the level after Introduction to Programming.

2. AI basics: The course assumes basic knowledge about machine learning, natural language processing, and reasoning. If you lack some of those, it may be good to work on this in the background. If you lack all of those, this will be an issue.

3. NLP: Students are assumed to have passed an introduction to NLP course, such as Text Mining.

Expectations of Student Participation

Attendance is strongly encouraged: quizzes and student presentations will be held in person only, and exam questions may be based on discussions during lectures.

4) Assessment

Group assessments (50%) will consist of two parts: student presentations (20%) throughout the course and a final project (30%). 

Project: Success on the final project relies on the skills from the course topics. The submission of the final assignment will consist of a blog tutorial-style notebook. The notebook will then be 'defended' in an oral examination with the teachers. Half of the points will come from the assignment notebook, whereas the other half will come from the examination. The final project grade must be at least 5/10. If a group scores below this grade, they can resubmit once based on feedback. Late submissions of projects will be penalized by 10% of the grade for each day of delay.

Presentations: Group presentations and projects will be carried out in groups of 3 students. 

The individual assessment (50%) will be split between quizzes (20%) and a final theory exam (30%). 

Exam: The final theory exam will be digital and will include a mix of multiple-choice and open-ended questions. Students must score at least 5/10 on the individual exam. If a student scores below this grade, they can retake the final theory exam. A sample exam will be published in the week before the exam.

Quizzes: Quizzes will be about practical aspects of conversational AI, and will primarily test student understanding of the weekly notebooks. Resitting quizzes is not possible. To accommodate inconveniences, we will consider the best 4 out of 5 quizzes for the overall grade. There is no minimum quiz grade.

Additional assessment notes:

The group assignments (project and presentations) are carried out in groups of 3 students. No group means no assignment submission (one person is not a group).

Compensation: You cannot compensate for one component with another.

The total grade, averaging the individual and the group assessment, must be at least 5.5/10.

We aim to notify you of each component's grades (final assignment, exam) within two working weeks from the submission date.

7) Use of AI and plagiarism

Note: It goes without saying that this section applies to both written work and presentations.

Use of generative AI is permitted in a limited way, with disclosure. In this course, generative AI (GenAI), such as ChatGPT or similar tools, may be used in a limited way, for example, for editorial support, rephrasing, or structure. Substantive contributions from GenAI may only be used within the limits set by the lecturer. Any use of GenAI must always be explicitly disclosed, in accordance with the lecturer's instructions. The student remains fully responsible for the content, quality, and accuracy of the submitted work. Incorrect use of GenAI is considered fraud. If established, the submitted work or assessment result will be declared invalid, and the Examination Board may impose further measures.

Plagiarism: Whenever you reuse the work of someone else, you need to attribute it explicitly. Do not copy-paste pieces of text. We use automated tools to check your work for plagiarism, but also machine-generated content is relatively easy to detect/sense. Every suspicion of fraud will be reported to the exam committee. Plagiarism means failing the course.
